Agentic AI Fraud Prevention

Bank of America Sr. Technical Product Manager / Program Lead 2022 – Present

An 8-agent LLM system that automates fraud operations at a top-tier bank. The engineering could have been built without a PM — but the technical PM framing the problem, choosing the evaluation criteria, and sequencing the rollout is what turned the technology into a production system.

1 The Problem

A rule-based fraud detection system was generating more noise than signal. Analysts were overwhelmed by false-positive alerts — and the recovery cost for flagged-but-legitimate transactions was roughly 4× the value of the actual fraud.

2 The Customers

Three distinct groups, each with a different definition of success:

  • Fraud analysts — the daily users. Needed fewer, higher-confidence alerts so they could focus on real risk.
  • Compliance and risk teams — the gatekeepers. Required explainability, auditability, and zero tolerance for false negatives.
  • Bank leadership — the sponsors. Needed a defensible business case: cost reduction without increased risk exposure.

3 The Solution I Owned

I owned the product vision — "reduce friction for legitimate users while improving fraud signal precision" — and the decisions that turned it into a shipped system.

What I specifically did:

  • Partnered with data science to identify where AI added real value versus where rules were already sufficient.
  • Sequenced an MVP that assisted analysts rather than replacing their decisions — earning trust before expanding autonomy.
  • Designed the multi-agent architecture: orchestration, inter-agent data contracts, retrieval, tool interfaces, and prompt governance.
  • Built the evaluation harness — rubric-based evals derived from human-reviewed SOPs — that turned production misses into prompt and logic improvements.

4 Results & Measurement

The system reduced false positives by 30%, improved fraud detection accuracy by 28%, improved operational efficiency by 50%, scaled automation to roughly 60% of manual operations, and prevented approximately $1M in indirect expense losses — all while maintaining regulatory confidence.

Proposed measurement approach — awaiting confirmation

For an AI fraud system, four dimensions need to be measured — it has to prove both that it catches fraud and that it doesn't disrupt legitimate users:

Model performance: recall, precision, false-positive rate, model drift over time.

Business impact: dollar value of fraud prevented, cost per investigation, alerts per analyst.

User experience: false-decline rate, support contact rate post-flag, time to resolution.

Operational: alert volume, % auto-resolved vs. manual review, feedback loop health.

Learning: For agentic AI systems, the evaluation harness — not the model — is the real moat. Teams that skip structured evals spend months tuning blind.
