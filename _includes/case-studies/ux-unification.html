04

UX Unification & Growth — Acquired Product Integration

Verisk Analytics Technical Product Manager 2018 – 2022

Brought an acquired analytics product onto Verisk's design standard without losing the customers attached to the old interface. A pure engineering team could have shipped the redesign — but the technical PM running the customer research, A/B testing, and stakeholder management is what kept adoption intact through the transition.

90%customer satisfaction

1 The Problem

Verisk had acquired multiple products over the years, each with its own interface, conventions, and customer base. The acquired analytics product looked and felt like a legacy tool. Users struggled with data visualization for quick decisions — a direct barrier to adoption and expansion.

The risk: long-tenured customers were attached to the old interface. A forced redesign without evidence would have driven churn.

2 The Customers

  • Mid-market insurer analysts — primary users; needed faster decision-making from data.
  • Enterprise customer stakeholders — attached to the existing interface; skeptical of change.
  • Verisk platform teams — needed the acquired product to conform to the company-wide design system for maintainability and cross-sell.

3 The Solution I Owned

  • Empathy mapping and customer interviews — surfaced what users actually needed versus what the old interface provided.
  • Design workshop with key customers, design leads, and tech leads — wireframes and mocks co-created, not imposed.
  • A/B testing — ran the old and new interfaces head-to-head with real user cohorts before full rollout.
  • Stakeholder management — convinced skeptical customers and internal teams using the A/B test data and user-interview findings.

4 Results & Measurement

Customer satisfaction rose to 90% after normalizing the acquired product onto Verisk's design standard. The UX work was part of the same broader initiative that took the product to $97M+ in revenue over four years.

Measurement framework

Satisfaction: customer satisfaction before and after the redesign.

Adoption & retention: active usage by customer segment and churn rate through the migration.

Learning: Bring compliance and security stakeholders into the design process early. Late-stage requirements are the most expensive kind of rework.
