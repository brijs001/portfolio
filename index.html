01

Agentic AI Fraud Prevention

Bank of America Sr. Technical Product Manager / Program Lead 2022 – Present

An 8-agent LLM system that automates fraud operations at a top-tier bank. The engineering could have been built without a PM — but the technical PM framing the problem, choosing the evaluation criteria, and sequencing the rollout is what turned the technology into a production system.

30%reduction in false positives
28%improvement in detection accuracy
50%operational efficiency gain
$1Mindirect expense losses prevented

1 The Problem

A rule-based fraud detection system was generating more noise than signal. Analysts were overwhelmed by false-positive alerts — and the recovery cost for flagged-but-legitimate transactions was roughly 4× the value of the actual fraud.

2 The Customers

Three distinct groups, each with a different definition of success:

  • Fraud analysts — the daily users. Needed fewer, higher-confidence alerts so they could focus on real risk.
  • Compliance and risk teams — the gatekeepers. Required explainability, auditability, and zero tolerance for false negatives.
  • Bank leadership — the sponsors. Needed a defensible business case: cost reduction without increased risk exposure.

3 The Solution I Owned

I owned the product vision — "reduce friction for legitimate users while improving fraud signal precision" — and the decisions that turned it into a shipped system.

What I specifically did:

  • Partnered with data science to identify where AI added real value versus where rules were already sufficient.
  • Sequenced an MVP that assisted analysts rather than replacing their decisions — earning trust before expanding autonomy.
  • Designed the multi-agent architecture: orchestration, inter-agent data contracts, retrieval, tool interfaces, and prompt governance.
  • Built the evaluation harness — rubric-based evals derived from human-reviewed SOPs — that turned production misses into prompt and logic improvements.

4 Results & Measurement

The system reduced false positives by 30%, improved fraud detection accuracy by 28%, improved operational efficiency by 50%, scaled automation to roughly 60% of manual operations, and prevented approximately $1M in indirect expense losses — all while maintaining regulatory confidence.

Proposed measurement approach — awaiting confirmation

For an AI fraud system, four dimensions need to be measured — it has to prove both that it catches fraud and that it doesn't disrupt legitimate users:

Model performance: recall, precision, false-positive rate, model drift over time.

Business impact: dollar value of fraud prevented, cost per investigation, alerts per analyst.

User experience: false-decline rate, support contact rate post-flag, time to resolution.

Operational: alert volume, % auto-resolved vs. manual review, feedback loop health.

Learning: For agentic AI systems, the evaluation harness — not the model — is the real moat. Teams that skip structured evals spend months tuning blind.

02

Trading Platform Modernization & Client Activation

Bank of America Sr. Technical Product Manager / Program Lead 2022 – Present

A modernization of a mission-critical trading platform handling 2M+ orders per hour. I owned specific customer journeys — authentication, order management, execution experience, APIs, and operational workflows. The migration could have been run as a pure engineering program — but the technical PM owning the customer-journey lens, prioritization, and cross-functional trade-offs is what made the difference between shipping infrastructure and delivering business outcomes.

60%reduction in feature delivery time
2M/hrorders supported at market peak
100+APIs in the partner-facing ecosystem
50%reduction in scope changes

1 The Problem

A legacy mainframe-based trading platform consumed 35% of engineering capacity in maintenance and was approaching end-of-life support — creating cyber exposure and blocking feature velocity. User-level churn was rising at 10%, driven by latency volatility during market spikes and a rigid UI/UX that slowed task completion.

Deployment cycles created constant tension between agility and regulatory safety — compliance required slower, more careful releases that frustrated product and business stakeholders.

2 The Customers

  • Institutional investors — hedge funds, asset managers, broker-dealers — executing high-frequency trades via FIX and REST APIs. Wanted self-service onboarding and real-time order visibility.
  • Retail partners — third-party brokerage platforms routing orders on behalf of end users. Needed reliable, low-latency integrations.
  • Internal teams — trading advisors, compliance, fraud analytics, and customer service — each a stakeholder in the platform's reliability and auditability.

3 The Solution I Owned

I owned specific customer journeys across the platform and ran a three-workstream strategy prioritized against business value and regulatory constraints:

  • Trading backend: built a sandbox + API Dev Portal for partners, cutting friction in test onboarding. Added telemetry to track partner errors and automate alerting. Led a UI redesign that introduced real-time log access with role-based visibility and contextual filters — cutting log retrieval time to under 60 seconds, helping reduce onboarding time by 75%.
  • Fraud detection integration: introduced an LLM-driven fraud scoring system — starting with a POC trained on historical triage logs. Worked with ML leads to define interpretability requirements to pass compliance scrutiny.
  • User interface and performance: shipped an interactive, customizable, widget-style UI for individual investors — 78% of adoption came from this work.
  • API-first modernization: decomposed legacy services into API-first microservices behind the partner-facing portal, and migrated key capabilities to AWS.
  • Self-service onboarding: automated validations across Operations, Compliance, and Technology that had previously been manual hand-offs.

I made the trade-offs: sequencing onboarding modernization first (fastest path to revenue), defending compliance requirements engineering wanted to defer, and keeping the UI redesign in scope when it would have been easier to cut.

4 Results & Measurement

Feature delivery time dropped by 60%, platform scalability now supports roughly 2 million orders per hour at market peak, and scope changes dropped by 50%. Client onboarding time fell from six weeks to under five days.

Proposed measurement approach — awaiting confirmation

Each workstream had its own success criteria because "external users" and "internal users" wanted different things:

External users — latency, uptime, self-service onboarding completion rate, API adoption.

Internal users — control, traceability, regulatory audit readiness, operational defect rate.

Business — feature delivery time, scope-change rate, client churn during migration.

Learning: Instrument partner-facing telemetry from day one. Partner pain points that surface in support tickets for months are the ones a real-time dashboard would have caught immediately.

03

Circular Navigator — 0-to-1 SaaS Analytics Platform

Verisk Analytics Technical Product Manager 2018 – 2022

Took an acquired product from launch to $97M+ in revenue over 4 years. The acquisition gave Verisk the technology — but the technical PM doing the discovery, segmentation, and pricing work is what scaled it beyond a single-tier product for a narrow slice of the insurance market.

$97M+revenue over 4 years
2.8×customer accounts in one year (50 → 140)
60%revenue growth in year one
3 tierssubscription pricing model launched

1 The Problem

Verisk acquired an Irish startup with a data product that tracked insurance-industry activity — speeding tickets, claims, regulatory changes — for insurers like USAA, AAA, and GEICO. The product had strong data but no product strategy: one pricing tier, one customer segment, and no clear path to scale.

The market opportunity was real but unaddressed: small-to-mid-market insurers were underserved by enterprise-grade analytics tools priced for Fortune 100 carriers. Vision: double customer adoption and grow revenue.

2 The Customers

  • Enterprise insurers — USAA, AAA, GEICO — needed deep data access and custom integrations.
  • Mid-market insurers — underserved by existing tools; needed the same insights at a lower price point with faster time-to-value.
  • Technical teams at insurer clients — wanted raw API access to pipe data into their own ecosystems, not just a UI.

Customers were paying full price for features they never used. Different customers had fundamentally different needs — but we were selling them the same product. I validated this with the data science team.

3 The Solution I Owned

I ran a two-phase discovery-to-launch process:

  • Problem discovery: competitive analysis to identify the SMB gap, plus customer interviews that surfaced two pain points — UI limitations slowing decisions, and pricing misaligned to actual usage.
  • Solution discovery: redesigned UI with a design workshop (wireframes, mocks, key customers, design and tech leads), and launched a three-tier subscription model based on usage-pattern segmentation.
  • Go-to-market: partnered with sales and marketing for insurance-industry conference promotion, channel sales to Verisk's existing customer base, and a one-month free trial.
  • Later pivot: identified the API-as-a-Service opportunity — opened direct API access for technical clients, creating an entirely new revenue stream.

4 Results & Measurement

In one year, customer accounts grew 2.8× (50 → 140) and revenue grew by 60%. Over four years, the product generated $97M+ in revenue.

Proposed measurement approach — awaiting confirmation

Adoption: new customers onboarded through each sales and marketing channel.

Conversion: customers moving from basic to premium tiers.

Churn: customers lost ÷ total customers.

CAC: sales & marketing cost ÷ new customers acquired.

Learning: Tiered pricing can cannibalize adjacent subscription products — worth accepting to reach a new segment, but build the migration path for overlapping customers up front.

04

UX Unification & Growth — Acquired Product Integration

Verisk Analytics Technical Product Manager 2018 – 2022

Brought an acquired analytics product onto Verisk's design standard without losing the customers attached to the old interface. A pure engineering team could have shipped the redesign — but the technical PM running the customer research, A/B testing, and stakeholder management is what kept adoption intact through the transition.

90%customer satisfaction

1 The Problem

Verisk had acquired multiple products over the years, each with its own interface, conventions, and customer base. The acquired analytics product looked and felt like a legacy tool. Users struggled with data visualization for quick decisions — a direct barrier to adoption and expansion.

The risk: long-tenured customers were attached to the old interface. A forced redesign without evidence would have driven churn.

2 The Customers

  • Mid-market insurer analysts — primary users; needed faster decision-making from data.
  • Enterprise customer stakeholders — attached to the existing interface; skeptical of change.
  • Verisk platform teams — needed the acquired product to conform to the company-wide design system for maintainability and cross-sell.

3 The Solution I Owned

  • Empathy mapping and customer interviews — surfaced what users actually needed versus what the old interface provided.
  • Design workshop with key customers, design leads, and tech leads — wireframes and mocks co-created, not imposed.
  • A/B testing — ran the old and new interfaces head-to-head with real user cohorts before full rollout.
  • Stakeholder management — convinced skeptical customers and internal teams using the A/B test data and user-interview findings.

4 Results & Measurement

Customer satisfaction rose to 90% after normalizing the acquired product onto Verisk's design standard. The UX work was part of the same broader initiative that took the product to $97M+ in revenue over four years.

Proposed measurement approach — awaiting confirmation

Satisfaction: customer satisfaction before and after the redesign.

Adoption & retention: active usage by customer segment and churn rate through the migration.

Learning: Bring compliance and security stakeholders into the design process early. Late-stage requirements are the most expensive kind of rework.
